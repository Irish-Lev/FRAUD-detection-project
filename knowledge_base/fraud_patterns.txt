KNOWN FRAUD PATTERNS — PAYMENT CARD FRAUD
Irish-Lev | Based on Sparkov EDA findings + Stripe fraud ops experience
Source: Phase 1 EDA + domain knowledge

================================================================
PATTERN 1 — CARD-NOT-PRESENT (CNP) FRAUD
================================================================

DEFINITION:
Online purchases made with stolen card details without the physical card.
The merchant cannot verify the card is present — only the card number,
expiry, and CVV are required, all of which can be stolen digitally.

SIGNALS IN THE SPARKOV DATASET:
- category = shopping_net or misc_net (highest fraud rates in EDA)
- geo_distance_km: often high — fraudster may be anywhere in the world
- hour_of_day: elevated during 00:00–04:00 (fraudsters automate scripts)
- amt: often above average — CNP fraud targets higher-value purchases
- velocity_24h: can be moderate — fraudster may test then buy

REAL-WORLD CONTEXT (Stripe):
CNP fraud is the dominant fraud type at online payment platforms like Stripe.
When card networks shifted to chip-and-PIN for in-person transactions,
fraudsters migrated online. "Shopping_net" and "misc_net" categories in
the Sparkov dataset directly correspond to this CNP risk profile.

HOW THE MODEL CATCHES IT:
XGBoost + FinBERT embeddings of merchant name + category text.
FinBERT encodes semantic signals from merchant names — "fraud_Rippin Kub"
patterns in Sparkov correspond to the kind of suspicious merchant strings
that flag in real payment processing pipelines.

================================================================
PATTERN 2 — CARD-TESTING FRAUD
================================================================

DEFINITION:
Fraudster makes many small transactions to verify a stolen card is active
before attempting large purchases. Often fully automated.

SIGNALS IN THE SPARKOV DATASET:
- velocity_24h: HIGH (5+ transactions in 24 hours — engineered in Phase 2)
- amt: very small (under $5-10 per transaction)
- category: often gas_transport or grocery_pos (small expected amounts)
- hour_of_day: often overnight — automated scripts run 24/7
- geo_distance_km: variable — fraudster may test near their location first

REAL-WORLD CONTEXT (Stripe):
Card-testing is extremely common. Fraudsters buy or steal card lists in
bulk, then run automated scripts against merchant APIs to check which
cards are live. Gas stations and grocery stores are prime targets because
small amounts do not trigger alerts and are easy to explain.

HOW THE MODEL CATCHES IT:
velocity_24h is a key engineered feature from Phase 2.
High velocity + small amount + gas or grocery category = card-testing signal.
SHAP will show velocity_24h as a top feature for these cases.

================================================================
PATTERN 3 — ACCOUNT TAKEOVER (ATO)
================================================================

DEFINITION:
Fraudster accesses a legitimate cardholder's account and makes purchases.
The card itself is valid; the fraudster has stolen login credentials.

SIGNALS IN THE SPARKOV DATASET:
- geo_distance_km: VERY HIGH — fraudster is in a different location
  than the cardholder's registered home address
- hour_of_day: often odd hours — cardholder is asleep
- category: different from the cardholder's normal spending pattern
- amt: can be high — fraudster wants maximum value before card is blocked
- is_night: often 1 (overnight transactions)

REAL-WORLD CONTEXT (Stripe):
Account takeover is particularly damaging because the card is legitimate.
The signal is the behavioral anomaly — the account suddenly behaves like
a different person. Geographic distance is the strongest single signal.

HOW THE MODEL CATCHES IT:
geo_distance_km (haversine distance between cardholder home and merchant)
engineered in Phase 2 is expected to be the top or second SHAP feature.
A card registered in Barcelona suddenly used at a merchant in New York
(high geo_distance) at 3am (is_night) is a near-certain ATO pattern.

================================================================
PATTERN 4 — GEOGRAPHIC ANOMALY (IMPOSSIBLE TRAVEL)
================================================================

DEFINITION:
Card used in two geographically distant locations within a short time window,
making the travel physically impossible. Strong indicator of card duplication
or simultaneous use by fraudster and cardholder.

SIGNALS IN THE SPARKOV DATASET:
- geo_distance_km: EXTREME (500km+ from registered home address)
- This is the most direct signal for this pattern type

REAL-WORLD CONTEXT:
If a card is used in Barcelona at 14:00 and then at a merchant 800km away
at 16:00, that is physically impossible — the cardholder cannot have
travelled that distance in 2 hours. This triggers immediate block at Stripe.

HOW THE MODEL CATCHES IT:
geo_distance_km > 500 is a strong fraud signal that SHAP will surface.
The EDA found geographic patterns in state-level fraud rates, confirming
that location matters significantly in this dataset.

================================================================
PATTERN 5 — STRUCTURING / AMOUNT SPLITTING
================================================================

DEFINITION:
Multiple transactions just below reporting or alert thresholds to avoid
detection. Designed to look like normal spending behaviour.

SIGNALS IN THE SPARKOV DATASET:
- velocity_24h: ELEVATED (multiple transactions close together)
- amt: repeated similar amounts just below round numbers ($99, $199, $499)
- category: often the same category repeatedly

REAL-WORLD CONTEXT:
Structuring is well-known in AML (anti-money laundering) contexts but
also appears in card fraud. Fraudsters have learned which amount thresholds
trigger enhanced scrutiny and deliberately transact below them.

HOW THE MODEL CATCHES IT:
Combination of velocity_24h + amount pattern. The autoencoder anomaly
detector (Tier 1 enhancement) is particularly well-suited to detecting
this pattern because it deviates from the normal distribution in a subtle
way that XGBoost alone may miss.

================================================================
PATTERN 6 — FALSE POSITIVE EXAMPLES (LEGITIMATE HIGH-RISK TRANSACTIONS)
================================================================

NOT ALL HIGH-SCORE TRANSACTIONS ARE FRAUD. Understanding false positives
is as important as detecting fraud — false positives hurt merchants and
degrade cardholder trust.

COMMON LEGITIMATE HIGH-RISK PATTERNS:
- Travel purchases: high amount + distant merchant = legitimate if category = travel
  A cardholder booking a hotel 1000km away is expected behaviour for travel category
- Business expenses: large amounts in entertainment or dining categories
- Holiday spending: velocity spikes around Christmas and New Year are legitimate
- Relocation: geo_distance_km suddenly increases when cardholder moves home

THRESHOLD TUNING IMPLICATION:
These patterns are why adaptive threshold tuning (Tier 1 enhancement)
is critical. A single global threshold of 0.5 will catch fraud but also
block legitimate transactions, costing the merchant money and trust.

The travel category specifically requires a higher fraud threshold —
the EDA showed travel has variable fraud rates but high legitimate amounts.
