================================================================
FEATURE: geo_distance_km
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
The straight-line distance in kilometres between the cardholder's registered
home address (lat/long columns) and the merchant location (merch_lat/merch_long).
Calculated using the Haversine formula to account for Earth's curvature.
Engineered in Phase 2 (02_feature_engineering.ipynb).

WHY IT MATTERS:
Legitimate cardholders transact near where they live and work.
Fraud typically involves a mismatch — the fraudster is in a different
location from the registered cardholder address.

FRAUD SIGNAL THRESHOLDS:
- Under 50km: typical legitimate range (home, work, local shops)
- 50–200km: elevated — possible travel but worth noting with other signals
- 200–500km: high risk — unusual for everyday spending
- 500km+: very high risk — strong geographic anomaly signal

EXPECTED SHAP IMPACT:
geo_distance_km is expected to be the top or second most important SHAP
feature based on the EDA finding that geographic patterns are highly
discriminative in this dataset. High geo_distance pushes SHAP value
positive (toward fraud). Low geo_distance pushes negative (toward legit).

REAL-WORLD CONTEXT (Stripe):
Geographic distance is one of the most reliable fraud signals in production.
A card registered in one city suddenly used in another country is an
immediate flag. Real-time geo checks run in under 50ms at production scale.

================================================================
FEATURE: hour_of_day
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
The hour (0–23) when the transaction was made.
Extracted from trans_date_trans_time in Phase 2.

WHY IT MATTERS:
The EDA (Cell 7) showed fraud rate spikes sharply between midnight and 4am —
approximately 3-4x the average fraud rate during these hours.
Legitimate cardholders sleep; automated fraud scripts run 24/7.

FRAUD SIGNAL THRESHOLDS:
- Hours 8–20: normal business hours — lower fraud baseline
- Hours 20–22: elevated but common for evening shopping
- Hours 22–24 and 0–6: high-risk window — elevated fraud rate confirmed in EDA
- Hours 0–4: HIGHEST risk — peak automated fraud activity

EXPECTED SHAP IMPACT:
High hour values (late night) push SHAP value positive (toward fraud).
Hours 8-18 (business hours) push SHAP value negative (toward legit).
Effect is stronger when combined with high geo_distance and online category.

================================================================
FEATURE: velocity_24h
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
The number of transactions on the same card in the 24 hours preceding
the current transaction. Engineered in Phase 2 using a rolling time window.

WHY IT MATTERS:
Normal cardholders make 1–3 transactions per day on average.
Card-testing fraud involves many rapid small transactions to verify cards.
Account takeover involves a sudden burst of purchases before the card is blocked.

FRAUD SIGNAL THRESHOLDS:
- 1–3 transactions: normal daily spending
- 4–6 transactions: slightly elevated — possible shopping day
- 7–10 transactions: HIGH — unusual velocity, card-testing likely
- 10+ transactions: VERY HIGH — strong automated fraud signal

EXPECTED SHAP IMPACT:
High velocity_24h pushes SHAP value strongly positive (toward fraud).
Combined with small amounts (card-testing pattern) the effect is amplified.

================================================================
FEATURE: log_amt
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
The log-transformed transaction amount — log1p(amt).
The EDA (Cell 5) showed raw amount is right-skewed with outliers.
Log transformation makes the distribution more symmetric for tree models.

WHY IT MATTERS:
Transaction amount is a weak standalone predictor (low linear correlation
confirmed in Cell 10 EDA) but important in combination with other features.
Very small amounts + high velocity = card-testing.
Very large amounts + online category + night hour = CNP fraud attempt.

FRAUD SIGNAL CONTEXT:
Amount alone is not enough — a $500 grocery transaction looks suspicious
but a $500 travel transaction is expected. The power of log_amt comes
from its interaction with category, geo_distance, and hour in the model.

================================================================
FEATURE: is_night
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
Binary flag — 1 if the transaction occurred between 22:00 and 06:00, else 0.
Derived from hour_of_day in Phase 2.

WHY IT MATTERS:
Captures the high-risk overnight window identified in the EDA.
Provides a clean binary signal on top of the continuous hour_of_day feature.

EXPECTED SHAP IMPACT:
is_night = 1 pushes SHAP positive (toward fraud).
Effect is strongest when combined with high geo_distance and online category.

================================================================
FEATURE: age
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
The cardholder's age at the time of the transaction.
Calculated as transaction year minus birth year (from dob column).
Engineered in Phase 2.

WHY IT MATTERS:
Different age groups have different spending patterns.
Certain fraud types target specific demographics more heavily.
Age also affects spending norms — an unusually large transaction for
a given age group raises the anomaly signal.

================================================================
FEATURE: category_encoded
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
The merchant category label-encoded to a numeric value.
14 unique categories in the Sparkov dataset.
Label encoding chosen over one-hot because XGBoost handles ordinal encoding
well and keeps the feature matrix compact.

WHY IT MATTERS:
The EDA (Cell 6) showed category is one of the most discriminative features.
shopping_net and misc_net have the highest fraud rates.
grocery_pos and gas_transport have lower rates but specific patterns
(card-testing with small amounts).

EXPECTED SHAP IMPACT:
Online categories (shopping_net, misc_net) push SHAP strongly positive.
In-person everyday categories (grocery, gas) push SHAP negative.
Travel is ambiguous — high amounts but legitimate travel is common.

================================================================
FEATURE: is_weekend
================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
Binary flag — 1 if the transaction occurred on Saturday or Sunday, else 0.
Derived from day_of_week in Phase 2.

WHY IT MATTERS:
Weekend spending patterns differ from weekday. Some fraud patterns
cluster on weekends when monitoring teams are at reduced capacity.

================================================================
INTERPRETING A SHAP WATERFALL PLOT FOR AN OPS AGENT
================================================================

When the Streamlit app shows a SHAP waterfall chart, each bar represents
one feature's contribution to the fraud score.

RED BARS (positive SHAP values) = pushing TOWARD fraud
BLUE BARS (negative SHAP values) = pushing TOWARD legitimate

HOW TO READ IT AS AN ANALYST:
"This transaction was scored 87% fraud probability.
The top contributors were:
- geo_distance_km = 840km (RED, strong) — the merchant is 840km from the
  cardholder's home, which is far outside normal spending range
- hour_of_day = 2am (RED, moderate) — this is peak automated fraud hours
- category = shopping_net (RED, moderate) — online retail is highest fraud category
- log_amt = $1,240 (RED, small) — amount is elevated above cardholder average

These three signals together match the Card-Not-Present pattern:
large online purchase, distant merchant, middle of the night."

This is the kind of context an analyst needs to make a fast, confident
decision when opening a Jira ticket — not just a score.
