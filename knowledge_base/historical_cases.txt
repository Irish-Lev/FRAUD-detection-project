HISTORICAL FRAUD CASE EXAMPLES — FROM SPARKOV DATASET PATTERNS
Irish-Lev | Ironhack Data Science & ML Bootcamp | Barcelona 2026
Source: Phase 1 EDA findings + Stripe fraud ops knowledge

These cases are representative patterns derived from the Sparkov dataset EDA.
They are used by the RAG system to retrieve similar examples when explaining
why a specific transaction was flagged. The RAG system matches incoming
transaction details to the closest pattern and explains accordingly.

================================================================
CASE TYPE 1 — CLASSIC CARD-NOT-PRESENT (CNP) FRAUD
================================================================

PROFILE:
Category:        shopping_net (online retail)
Amount:          $800–$2,000 (high relative to average)
Hour:            01:00–04:00 (middle of night)
geo_distance_km: 500–1,200km (far from home)
velocity_24h:    2–4 (moderate — post card-test)
is_night:        1

FRAUD CONFIRMED — Pattern match: Card-Not-Present

EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT:
This transaction profile matches classic CNP fraud — a large online
purchase made in the middle of the night at a merchant located far from
the cardholder's home. The shopping_net category confirms card-not-present:
no physical card verification occurred. The distance of 500–1,200km from
the registered home address means the fraudster is operating remotely.
Recommend: call cardholder to verify, check if other recent transactions
exist on this card in a different city.

================================================================
CASE TYPE 2 — CARD-TESTING FOLLOWED BY LARGE PURCHASE
================================================================

PROFILE (card-testing phase):
Category:        gas_transport or grocery_pos
Amount:          $1.50–$9.99 (very small)
Hour:            any — often automated
geo_distance_km: low (fraudster may test at nearby merchant)
velocity_24h:    8–15 (very high — automated testing)

PROFILE (exploitation phase, same card):
Category:        shopping_net or misc_net
Amount:          $500–$3,000
Hour:            00:00–05:00
geo_distance_km: high (fraudster now using remotely)

FRAUD CONFIRMED — Pattern match: Card-Testing then Exploit

EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT:
The card shows a burst of small transactions at gas or grocery merchants
(typical card-testing behaviour) followed by a large online purchase.
The velocity_24h is elevated from the testing phase. This is automated
card verification — the fraudster ran a script to check the card was live,
then immediately used it for a high-value purchase. Recommend: block card,
check the full 24-hour transaction history, report to card network.

================================================================
CASE TYPE 3 — ACCOUNT TAKEOVER (ATO)
================================================================

PROFILE:
Category:        shopping_net, entertainment, or misc_net
Amount:          $300–$2,000 (large relative to cardholder history)
Hour:            22:00–04:00 (while cardholder is asleep)
geo_distance_km: 300–800km (fraudster in different city)
velocity_24h:    3–8 (fraudster buys multiple items quickly)
is_night:        1

FRAUD CONFIRMED — Pattern match: Account Takeover

EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT:
The cardholder's account is making purchases at a merchant located
hundreds of kilometres from their registered home, in the middle of the
night, with several transactions in quick succession. This is a strong
account takeover pattern — the cardholder's credentials were likely
compromised, and a fraudster in a different city is making purchases
before the cardholder wakes up and notices. Recommend: immediate card
block, call cardholder, initiate ATO review process.

================================================================
CASE TYPE 4 — GEOGRAPHIC ANOMALY (IMPOSSIBLE TRAVEL)
================================================================

PROFILE:
Category:        any
Amount:          any
Hour:            any
geo_distance_km: 800km+ (extreme distance from home)
Previous transaction: close to home within past 2–6 hours

FRAUD CONFIRMED — Pattern match: Geographic Anomaly

EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT:
The transaction is occurring at a merchant more than 800km from the
cardholder's registered home address. Combined with a recent transaction
near home, this represents an impossible travel scenario — the cardholder
cannot physically be in both locations. Either the card is being used by
a fraudster in a distant location simultaneously, or the cardholder has
recently moved without updating their address. Recommend: contact
cardholder immediately to verify location.

================================================================
CASE TYPE 5 — STRUCTURING (AMOUNT SPLITTING)
================================================================

PROFILE:
Category:        same category repeated
Amount:          $95–$99 or $195–$199 (just below round thresholds)
Hour:            any
geo_distance_km: low to moderate
velocity_24h:    5–12 (elevated but not extreme)

FRAUD CONFIRMED — Pattern match: Structuring

EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT:
Multiple transactions with amounts just below common alert thresholds
($100, $200) suggest structuring — the fraudster has learned which
amounts trigger enhanced scrutiny and is deliberately transacting below
them. The elevated velocity combined with suspiciously similar amounts
is the key signal. This pattern is harder to catch than obvious CNP
fraud and may require escalation to the AML team if a pattern persists.

================================================================
CASE TYPE 6 — FALSE POSITIVE: LEGITIMATE TRAVEL
================================================================

PROFILE:
Category:        travel
Amount:          $400–$1,500 (high but expected for travel)
Hour:            business hours (08:00–20:00)
geo_distance_km: 400–2,000km (high — cardholder is travelling)
velocity_24h:    1–3 (normal)

OUTCOME: LEGITIMATE — False positive risk

EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT:
Although the geo_distance is high and the amount is elevated, the
merchant category is "travel" which is expected to show high distances
and amounts. The transaction occurred during business hours with normal
velocity. This profile matches a cardholder who is travelling — booking
a hotel or flight from a location far from home is completely expected
behaviour. Recommend: clear the transaction unless other signals present.
The model may have a higher false positive rate on travel — threshold
should be adjusted upward for travel category.

================================================================
CASE TYPE 7 — FALSE POSITIVE: HOLIDAY SPENDING SPIKE
================================================================

PROFILE:
Category:        shopping_pos or shopping_net
Amount:          elevated relative to monthly average
Hour:            daytime
geo_distance_km: low (near home)
velocity_24h:    4–7 (higher than usual but cardholder is shopping)
Date:            late December or early January

OUTCOME: LEGITIMATE — Seasonal spike

EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT:
Elevated velocity and amount during holiday season (Christmas, New Year)
are expected. Cardholders make more purchases than usual during this
period. The low geo_distance (near home or local mall) and daytime hours
reduce the fraud risk significantly. The model may generate more false
positives in December-January due to spending pattern changes.
Recommend: clear unless other red flags are present.

HOW TO USE THESE CASES IN THE OPS WORKFLOW
================================================================

When the RAG system retrieves one of these case types, it means the
incoming flagged transaction closely matches the pattern described.

ANALYST ACTION FLOW:
1. Read the fraud score and case type returned by RAG
2. Check the SHAP waterfall for the specific feature values
3. Match against the case profile above
4. Use the "EXPLANATION FOR OPS AGENT" text as the basis for your
   call script when contacting the cardholder
5. Log your decision (confirmed fraud / false positive) in the system
   so the model can learn from real outcomes

The combination of XGBoost score + SHAP explanation + RAG case match
should allow an experienced ops agent to make a decision in 3–4 minutes
versus the 8–12 minutes typically required without AI assistance.
