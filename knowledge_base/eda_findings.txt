Fraud transactions:       ~7,506 in training set
Fraud rate:               0.5789% (severely imbalanced)

Average fraud amount:     significantly higher than legitimate
Average legitimate amount: lower, more consistent

================================================================
FINDING 1 — SEVERE CLASS IMBALANCE (MOST CRITICAL)
================================================================

Only 0.58% of all transactions are fraudulent.
For every 1 fraud transaction there are approximately 172 legitimate ones.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
- Accuracy is useless as a metric — a model predicting "legit" for
  everything gets 99.42% accuracy but catches ZERO fraud
- We use Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC) as our primary metric, NOT ROC-AUC
  ROC-AUC is optimistic under severe class imbalance and gives a
  misleadingly high score even for poor fraud models
- We apply SMOTE on the training set ONLY to oversample the minority class
  before model training — never on the full dataset (avoids data leakage)
- At Stripe in real production, this imbalance is even more extreme:
  fraud teams work queues of approximately 1 in 500-1000 transactions

IMPLICATION FOR MODEL EVALUATION:
When an agent or analyst sees a fraud flag, precision matters more than
recall in most ops contexts — a high false positive rate burns agent
time and degrades merchant trust. Threshold tuning is essential.

================================================================
FINDING 2 — TIME OF DAY HAS A STRONG FRAUD SIGNAL
================================================================

Fraud rate spikes sharply between midnight (00:00) and 4am.
The high-risk window (0–4am) shows approximately 3-4x the average fraud rate.

WHY THIS MATTERS OPERATIONALLY:
- Legitimate cardholders have sleep patterns; automated fraud bots do not
- Fraudsters often run scripts overnight to exploit reduced monitoring
- At Stripe, hour_of_day is one of the first features fraud rules teams
  reach for when writing velocity rules and alert thresholds
- This is a well-known signal across all major payment platforms

FEATURE ENGINEERING IMPLICATION:
- hour_of_day (0-23) is a core engineered feature in Phase 2
- is_night flag (binary: 1 if hour between 22:00-06:00) amplifies this signal
- day_of_week and is_weekend add additional temporal context

================================================================
FINDING 3 — MERCHANT CATEGORY IS HIGHLY DISCRIMINATIVE
================================================================

Fraud rates vary dramatically across merchant categories.
Online and card-not-present categories show the highest fraud rates.

HIGH RISK CATEGORIES (above average fraud rate):
- shopping_net: HIGHEST — card-not-present online purchases
  Physical card never verified; stolen card details work just as well
- misc_net: SECOND HIGHEST — unclassified online merchants
  Higher risk because merchant type is unclear (harder to model)
- entertainment: elevated — higher ticket sizes, harder to dispute
- shopping_pos: moderate — in-person but still targeted

LOWER RISK CATEGORIES:
- grocery_pos: lower — high volume but small amounts; card-testing risk
- gas_transport: lower — frequent target for card-testing small charges
- travel: variable — legitimate large amounts common; hard to flag
- health_fitness, personal_care: typically low fraud rates

STRIPE CONTEXT:
Card-not-present (CNP) fraud is the dominant fraud type at online payment
platforms. When a merchant category is "shopping_net" or "misc_net",
the fraud probability is elevated before we even look at the amount or
geographic distance. This is why FinBERT embeddings of merchant name +
category add genuine signal to the tabular model.

================================================================
FINDING 4 — GEOGRAPHIC PATTERNS
================================================================

Top 10 states by fraud rate show geographic clustering.
States with lower population density show higher per-capita fraud rates
in some categories — possibly because fewer in-person verification
checks occur in rural areas.

KEY INSIGHT:
Geographic distance between the cardholder's registered home address
and the merchant location is one of the strongest fraud signals available.
A transaction 800km from home at 2am in an online shopping category
is a completely different risk profile from a grocery store transaction
2km from home at 10am.

geo_distance_km (haversine distance between cardholder lat/long and
merchant merch_lat/merch_long) is engineered in Phase 2 and expected
to be a top SHAP feature after model training.

================================================================
FINDING 5 — TRANSACTION AMOUNT PATTERNS
================================================================

Distribution shape:
- Raw amount: right-skewed, dominated by a few large values
- Log-transformed amount: more symmetrical, better for tree models
- Fraud amounts: shifted right — fraud transactions tend to be larger
  on average, but with exceptions (card-testing involves very small amounts)

CARD-TESTING PATTERN:
Fraudsters make many small transactions (under $5-10) to verify a stolen
card is active before attempting large purchases.
Signal: very small amounts + high velocity_24h (many txns in 24 hours)

LARGE AMOUNT PATTERN:
Once a card is verified as active, fraudsters attempt large purchases.
Signal: amount significantly above the cardholder's historical average,
combined with an online merchant category and distant location.

log_amt (log1p transformation of amount) is used as a feature in Phase 2
to capture the amount signal without the raw skew causing model issues.

================================================================
CORRELATION FINDINGS (Cell 10)
================================================================

Numeric features examined: amt, city_pop, hour, is_fraud

Key observations:
- amt shows weak positive correlation with is_fraud — amount alone is not
  a strong standalone predictor but contributes in combination with others
- city_pop shows very weak correlation — population of cardholder city
  is not a primary fraud signal on its own
- hour shows weak but non-zero correlation with is_fraud — confirms the
  time-of-day signal observed in Cell 7

IMPORTANT NOTE:
Low linear correlation does NOT mean a feature is useless. XGBoost finds
non-linear relationships. hour_of_day has low linear correlation but is
a strong signal when combined with category and geo_distance.
This is why we use tree-based models, not linear models, as our primary.
